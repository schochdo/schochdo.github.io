Basin Inflow Sources (12.85 MAF Runoff)

Basin runoff originates as high-elevation snowpack in Colorado, Wyoming, and Utah (12.40 MAF) plus Lower Basin tributaries (0.45 MAF from the Virgin and Little Colorado). Lake Powell buffers upstream snowmelt to release 7.90 MAF past Lees Ferry into Lake Mead.

Annual Storage Drawdown (2.05 MAF)

The red band entering the Lower Basin represents annual storage drawdown. Lower Basin consumptive use, Mexico treaty deliveries, and reservoir evaporation total 10.40 MAF per year, while Lees Ferry releases average 7.90 MAF and Lower tributaries add 0.45 MAF. The basin bridges this 2.05 MAF deficit by drawing down storage in Lake Mead.

Consumptive Fate & Evaporation (14.90 MAF)

Water splits between agriculture (8.72 MAF) (Upper Basin Ag 3.90, Imperial & Coachella 3.40, Arizona Ag 1.42), municipal drinking water (3.28 MAF) (Phoenix/Tucson 0.90, SoCal MWD 1.00, Front Range 0.48, Las Vegas 0.28, Baja 0.25, Wasatch 0.12), the 1944 Mexico Treaty (1.50 MAF), and reservoir evaporation & transit losses (1.40 MAF).

⚠ +3.90 MAF Structural Over-Allocation

Paper allocations (19.10 MAF) exceed average 20th-century natural runoff (15.20 MAF) by 3.90 MAF. Under modern aridification (~12.50 MAF), the structural overdraft expands to +6.60 MAF/yr, forcing continuous reservoir drawdown.

The Gila River Legal Paradox

The Gila River Basin produces ~0.87 MAF of natural runoff, but is virtually 100% intercepted upstream for Phoenix and the Salt River Valley. In Arizona v. California (1963), the U.S. Supreme Court confirmed the Gila is excluded from mainstream Compact accounting, giving Arizona exclusive tributary use on top of its 2.8 MAF Colorado River quota.

Lake Powell & Lake Mead Buffers

Lake Powell buffers upstream snowpack volatility to guarantee Lee's Ferry releases. Lake Mead buffers Lower Basin deliveries to California, Arizona, Nevada, and Mexico. When releases exceed inflows, combined storage drops, creating the post-2026 cliff.
