Statutory entitlements (17.50 MAF) plus CY 2000–2024 measured mainstem reservoir evaporation (1.32 MAF) reach 18.82 MAF/yr—exceeding 2000–2024 mainstream natural inflows (12.76 MAF/yr: 12.40 Upper + 0.36 Lower gaged tribs) by +6.06 MAF/yr (+4.74 MAF/yr against statutory-only claims). For reference, the 1922–2024 post-Compact Lees Ferry natural flow average is 14.11 MAF/yr.

Gila River vs. Lower Mainstem Tributaries

Virgin, Little Colorado, Bill Williams, Muddy, and Paria deliver 0.36 MAF/yr (USGS CY 2000–2024) directly into the Colorado mainstem below Lees Ferry. By contrast, the Gila River Basin (1.99 MAF/yr USBR CY 2000–2024 Tributary CUL) is fully intercepted in central Arizona and New Mexico; under Arizona v. California (1963), it is excluded from Compact mainstream accounting.

How 29.8 MAF of Storage Closed the 2000–2024 Budget

Although statutory claims exceed 2000–2024 inflows by 6.06 MAF/yr, Upper Basin states actually consumed 3.90 MAF/yr (4.37 MAF/yr including CRSP mainstem evaporation) instead of their 7.50 MAF paper cap. Actual depletions of 14.14 MAF/yr were balanced by draining 29.8 MAF from the 5 federal reservoirs between 1999-12-31 (51.8 MAF) and 2024-12-31 (22.0 MAF)—an average drawdown of -1.19 MAF/yr (Mead -0.65, Powell -0.51, CRSP -0.03 MAF/yr).

Natural Flow vs. Compact Promises (Water Years 1922–2024)

The 16.5 MAF core statutory promise (7.5 Upper + 7.5 Lower + 1.5 Mexico) exceeded official USBR Lees Ferry Natural Flow in 68.0% of years—and 21 of 25 years since 2000—forcing the system to draw down Powell and Mead storage to cover the gap.
