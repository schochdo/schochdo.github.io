1. Statutory Compacts & International Treaties (Paper Water Allocations)

The foundational legal compacts dividing interstate entitlements across the seven basin states and the Republic of Mexico

Rate Bridge: 1 MAF/yr ≈ 1,381.16 cfs
1922 Colorado River Compact 42 Stat. 171
7.50 MAF Upper • 7.50 MAF Lower

Article III(a) apportioned exclusive beneficial consumptive use of 7.50 MAF/yr each in perpetuity. Art. III(d) mandates the Upper Division states shall not cause the flow at Lees Ferry to be depleted below 75.0 MAF over any consecutive 10-year period.

1948 Upper Basin Compact 63 Stat. 31
CO 51.75% • UT 23% • WY 14% • NM 11.25%

Article III apportions the variable Upper Basin water pool after Arizona's fixed 50,000 AF apportionment: Colorado diverts 51.75% (2.33 MAF), Utah 23.0% (1.04 MAF), Wyoming 14.0% (0.63 MAF), and New Mexico 11.25% (0.50 MAF) based on current ~4.50 MAF consumption.

Upper CO River Commission (UCRC) ↗
1928 BCPA & Decree 373 U.S. 546
CA 4.40 MAF • AZ 2.80 MAF • NV 0.30 MAF

Boulder Canyon Project Act § 4(a) and the landmark 1964 Supreme Court Decree in Arizona v. California divide the normal 7.50 MAF Lower Basin mainstream entitlement among California (4.40 MAF), Arizona (2.80 MAF), and Nevada (0.30 MAF).

USBR Decree Accounting Office ↗
1944 US-Mexico Treaty TS 994
1.50 MAF/yr Guaranteed Delivery

Treaty Series 994 commits the United States to deliver 1.50 MAF annually to Mexico at the international boundary. Diverted at Morelos Dam into the Canal Alimentador Central: Mexicali Valley agriculture receives 1.25 MAF, and Baja California cities receive 0.25 MAF.

2. Macro Accounting Datasets & Regional Conduits (Physical Water Accounting)

Official annual accounting ledgers and regional infrastructure authorities measuring physical diversions, return flows, and evaporative losses

Lower Basin Article V Decree Accounting
USBR Boulder Canyon Operations • Annual Water Accounting

Published annually under Article V of the Supreme Court Decree. Provides exact metered diversion records, return flow credits, and net consumptive use for every entitlement holder across California (4.40 MAF), Arizona (2.80 MAF), and Nevada (0.30 MAF).

Repository: USBR Decree Accounting ↗
Upper Basin Consumptive Uses & Losses (CULR)
USBR Upper Colorado Region & UCRC

Official federal accounting of agricultural evapotranspiration, municipal trans-basin exports, and reservoir evaporation across Colorado (2.33 MAF), Utah (1.04 MAF), Wyoming (0.63 MAF), and New Mexico (0.50 MAF), totaling current ~4.50 MAF consumptive depletions.

Repository: USBR CULR Portal ↗
Natural Virgin Flow & Storage Overdraft
USBR Natural Flow Database (1906–Present)

Unimpaired virgin runoff modeled across 29 natural flow nodes: 12.40 MAF Upper Basin + 0.45 MAF Lower tributaries = 12.85 MAF basin total. Total depletions (14.90 MAF) exceed natural supply, actively bridging the 2.05 MAF deficit via multi-year storage drawdown from Lake Mead and Lake Powell.

Datasets: USBR Natural Flow ↗
California Conduits: All-American Canal & CRA (4.40 MAF)
Imperial Irrigation District (IID) • MWD of SoCal

All-American Canal diverts 3.40 MAF at Imperial Dam to irrigate 500,000 acres in Imperial and Coachella valleys. Colorado River Aqueduct lifts 1.00 MAF 1,617 ft across 242 miles of desert to serve 19 million coastal Southern California residents.

Agencies: IID ↗ • MWD ↗
Arizona Conduits: CAP & Mainstem Canals (2.80 MAF)
Central Arizona Project (CAP) • USBR Yuma Area Office

Central Arizona Project canal lifts 1.38 MAF across 336 miles to Phoenix, Tucson, and tribal nations. Senior on-river gravity canals in Yuma and Wellton-Mohawk divert 1.42 MAF for year-round agricultural production.

Authorities: CAP ↗ • USBR Yuma ↗
Nevada SNWA & Trans-Mountain Exports (0.90 MAF)
SNWA Intakes • Colorado DWR HydroBase • Utah CUP

SNWA diverts 0.50 MAF from Lake Mead, returning 0.20 MAF via Las Vegas Wash for 0.30 MAF net municipal consumption. Colorado Front Range tunnels export 0.48 MAF under the Continental Divide, and Utah CUP exports 0.12 MAF to the Wasatch Front.
