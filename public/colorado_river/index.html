Annual Deficit

The 1922 Compact negotiators assumed an average virgin flow of ~17.5–18.0 MAF during an anomalously wet decade (1905–1922). Modern hydrology establishes true sustainable yield at ~12.4 MAF, producing an annual structural overdraft of ~5.1 Million Acre-Feet.

THE LAW OF THE RIVER: COMPACT ALLOCATIONS

Statutory division of water rights across Upper & Lower Basins

1. The 1922 Colorado River Compact

Divided the watershed at Lee's Ferry, Arizona into the Upper Basin (CO, WY, UT, NM) and Lower Basin (AZ, CA, NV). Each basin was apportioned 7.5 MAF/yr of beneficial consumptive use in perpetuity. Lower Basin was granted the right to increase use by 1.0 MAF/yr under Article III(b).

2. 1944 Mexican Water Treaty

Guarantees Mexico 1.5 MAF/yr delivered at Morelos Dam. During surplus, up to 1.7 MAF; during extraordinary drought, reduced in proportion to US domestic reductions.

3. 1948 Upper Colorado River Basin Compact

Apportioned Upper Basin water on a percentage basis: Colorado (51.75%), Utah (23.00%), Wyoming (14.00%), New Mexico (11.25%), with Arizona receiving 50,000 AF/yr.

4. 1963 Arizona v. California Decree

Apportioned the Lower Basin's 7.5 MAF mainstream entitlement: California 4.4 MAF/yr, Arizona 2.8 MAF/yr, Nevada 0.3 MAF/yr.

Data Endpoints & Sources

  • USGS Water Services (NWIS): Real-time instantaneous discharge (00060) at Lee's Ferry, Cameo, Cisco, Bluff, and Hoover Dam reaches.
  • USBR Upper Colorado HydroData: Reservoir elevations and storage acre-feet for Powell (919), Flaming Gorge (917), Navajo (920).
  • USBR Lower Colorado Hourly Teletype: Lake Mead, Mohave, and Havasu operations.
  • USDA NRCS SNOTEL: Basin Snow Water Equivalent (SWE) percentiles and peak runoff indices.
